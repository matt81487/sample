Questions 3 through 8 are based on the following fact pattern:

Broke is widely known for being in the retail business of selling restaurants the Model A13 laptop
computer (‘computer”) at a price of $1,000 to $1,100 each. Broke purchases the computers directly
from foreign manufacturer, Hyum. Broke pays Hyum $500 for each computer. Broke keeps cash
reserves in the amount of $150,000 for inventory purchases. Hyum has virtually an endless supply
of computers to sell Broke for $500 each. Broke knowingly enters into sale agreements with third
party U.S. customers for the sale of the computers. It then purchases the computers from Hyum,
after entering a contract with the customer, and subsequently delivers the computers it receives
from Hyum to its customers. Andy’s Pancakes, one of Broke’s regular customers, breached a
contract to purchase 5 computers, for $1,000 each, by wrongfully refusing to accept five computers
when Broke attempted to deliver the computers. Broke then rerouted its delivery truck with the
computers it had earmarked for Andy’s Pancakes to ICS Builders, another one of Broke’s regular
customers. ICS purchased the five computers for $1,100 each. Broke sued Andy’s Pancakes for
damages.
