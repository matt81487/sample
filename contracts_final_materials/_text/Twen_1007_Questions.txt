Aranda Painting, Inc. contracts with TJS to paint its playground for 44000 dollars between Mar 1 and Mar 30th. It will cost Aranda 26000 dollars to perform the job with its regular team of 5 independent contractors. But if Aranda uses six independent contractors, the project is likely to cost 32000 dollars but would be completed much quicker, though Aranda has no plans to obtain a 6th contractor. Aranda typically uses contractors according to its workflow and has access to 50 independent contractors who are contractually on-call for Aranda. Shockingly, TJS repudiates the contract before Aranda starts performance on March 1. Aranda immediately starts a painting contract project with IBM to paint its business school for 52000 dollars from March 2-March 17 that requires 7 contractors. It will cost Aranda 30000 dollars to paint the business school. Aranda sues TJS for 44000 dollars damages. TJS argues Aranda has no damages because its IBM contract exceeds the TJS contract value. How much should a court award Aranda in expectation damages, if anything?
Mason County contracts with Builder to construct a clinic for 1 million dollars. After Builder has spent $150000 on labor/materials, Mason County clearly repudiates and instructs Builder to stop. Builder ignores the repudiation, completes the clinic at an additional cost of $850000, and sues Mason County for damages. Builder salvages $10000 worth of scrap from unused materials. What is Builder entitled to recover?
