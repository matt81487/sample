COURSE LEARNING OUTCOMES:

By the end of this course, the student should be able to: (1) understand the sources of Contract Law in the United States; (2) identify and evaluate the philosophical and jurisprudential assumptions that underlie Contract Law, such as theories of conditions, breach, performance, interpretation, and damages; (3) explain how such theories may influence the outcome of contractual relationships; (4) explain some of the practical “problems” that parties may encounter when attempting to perform contracts and how the enforcement of contract law may facilitate just or unjust results; (5) construct a persuasive legal argument as to how a contract dispute should be resolved; (6) explain the various policy concerns that make an otherwise unenforceable agreement binding; (7) answer complex hypothetical questions involving performance and justifications for nonperformance; and (8) explain the various justifications, excuses and defenses to contract nonperformance under common law and certain provisions of the Uniform Commercial Code.

SOCRATIC METHOD

Class is not designed for me to explain the assigned cases to you or demonstrate the depth of my knowledge about the subject matter.  Rather, the class session will be used for you to demonstrate the depth of your knowledge and for us to engage in the exchange of intellectually driven ideas that emanate from your analysis of the material facts, issues, rules, and theories discussed in the assigned cases.  Hence, the classroom experience will provide you with an opportunity to test and demonstrate your understanding of the rules, theories, and reasoning contained in the assigned case opinions.    

In order to provide you the best opportunity to demonstrate your understanding of the reading assignments, I may pose a series of questions to you. You are expected to correctly answer all of my questions.   I may not merely ask, "What happened?"  I may also ask, "What if…" If you are unable to respond properly to my questions, it may be an indication that you are not adequately prepared for class.  At times, I may ask you to respond to another student’s arguments or impressions regarding a case.  So active listening and decisiveness are important during the course.  Rarely, if ever, will, “I do not know” be an acceptable response to a question related to a case or rule. 

The reading assignments are subject to revision.  Indeed, our actual schedule may be slightly faster or slower.  We will, however, complete all of the assignments before the end of the semester and follow the syllabus.
