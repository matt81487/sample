Facts:
Dr. Victoria Javadi is a neurologist who was born and raised in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. After
completing her residency at UPMC, she joined The Pitt Medical Center as an attending
physician. In 2020, Dr. Javadi began working part-time with a national telemedicine network,
NeuroLink Health, which allows her to consult with patients remotely. Dr. Javadi lives in
Pittsburgh most of the year, but for part of each winter (December through March), she stays in
Austin, Texas, where her husband, Nurse Mateo Rios, works full-time at a children’s hospital.

In early 2023, Dr. Javadi learned from a former classmate that a Texas-based startup, MindMap
Tech, was promoting a new mental health app called FocusMe, featuring an image of a woman
in scrubs with dark curly hair, a stethoscope, and a distinctive birthmark identical to Dr. Javadi’s.
The image was used as the app’s clickable logo and appeared on marketing materials online.

The founder of MindMap Tech, Dr. Zuri Patel, operates the business from Austin, Texas, and
maintains a website, www.mindmaptech.com, where the app can be previewed and downloaded.
The site lists Dr. Patel’s Austin address and business email but does not include any physical
retail locations. Anyone in the U.S. can access the website, and the app has been downloaded
thousands of times, including by several users in Pennsylvania.

Dr. Javadi is upset. She never consented to the use of her likeness, and she believes this could
interfere with her future opportunities for media and medical brand endorsements. She is
considering filing a lawsuit for violation of her right of publicity and misappropriation of
likeness.

Pennsylvania’s long-arm statute provides: “The courts of this state have jurisdiction over any
person to the fullest extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution.”
Texas’s long-arm statute provides: “The courts of this state have jurisdiction over any
nonresident who transacts business in or commits a tort in this state. A nonresident is anyone
who lives outside of Texas for more than six months of any year.”

Question: Could Dr. Javadi successfully argue that a court in Pennsylvania has general
jurisdiction over Dr. Patel?

A. Maybe, but only if Dr. Patel’s website shows purposeful availment through the effects test.
B. Yes, but only if the case is filed in federal court.
C. Maybe, but only if Dr. Patel’s business activities in Pennsylvania are so substantial as to
render her essentially at home there.
D. Yes, because Dr. Patel is domiciled in Texas.
E. B and D are both correct.
