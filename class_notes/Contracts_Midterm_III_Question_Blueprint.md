## Exam format (per Prof. Jones)
- About 10 questions on **expectation, reliance, and restitution** (fill-in / calculation).
- **2-3 questions on assignment / third-party beneficiaries** (Herzog, Vogan, R302-311, R317-333).
- **NOT on Midterm III:** liquidated damages (Barrie School, R356) and specific performance. Specific performance is on the **final only**. Do not build these into Midterm III quizzes.

## Rules to test

### Substantial performance vs. total breach (Osteen v. Johnson) — professor asked for this specifically
- Restitution is available **only for a total (substantial) breach** that goes to the essence of the contract.
- **Substantial performance and total breach can't coexist.** If the breaching party substantially performed, the breach is minor, and the injured party gets **damages, not restitution**.
- Restitution amount when available = what the plaintiff paid − the reasonable value of the services the breaching party performed (Osteen: $2,500 − value of services; Garcia TWEN: $100,000 − $33,000 = **$67,000**).
- Include a question where there's been a breach **plus substantial performance**, so the answer is **no restitution**.

### Chicago Coliseum Club v. Dempsey
- Not recoverable: speculative lost profits (entertainment events lack the stability of regular business), pre-contract expenses, and the costs of an injunction / attorney's fees.
- Recoverable reliance: necessary expenses between the signing date and the repudiation date.

### Vogan v. Hayes Appraisal (R302, 304, 305, 306, 310, 311)
- Framework: (1) Does the contract benefit a non-party? (2) Can that person enforce it? Only an **intended**, not incidental, beneficiary can.
- The plaintiff must still prove causation and recoverable damages: the inaccurate reports directly caused the loss, and there are no speculative lost profits.

### Herzog v. Irace (R317, 318, 321, 322, 323, 324, 331, 332, 336)
- A delivered letter is an effective assignment and **can't be revoked**. After notice, the obligor holds the fund for the assignee.
- The lawyer was legally obligated to honor the assignment; client instructions don't override it.
