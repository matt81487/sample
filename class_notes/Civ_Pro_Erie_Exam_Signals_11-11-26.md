# Civ Pro: Erie exam signals (class 11/11/26)

Source: `Civ_Pro_Class_Notes_11-11-26_Erie.docx`. These notes feed future Civ Pro question sets.

## What the professor said will be tested
- **She won't ask you to *apply* the tests.** Expect conceptual calls instead:
  - **Why is Erie important?** It stops forum shopping and unequal (inequitable) administration of the laws. Federal and state courts now apply the *same* state law, so the result no longer depends on which court hears the case. Erie abolished "general federal common law" (Swift v. Tyson).
  - **What test would help the plaintiff argue X?** If the plaintiff wants **state** law, use the **outcome-determinative test** (does resolving the issue decide the whole case? Yes = substantive). If a party wants **federal** law, use Hanna (a valid Federal Rule on point) or Byrd balancing.
- **Substantive vs. procedural:** a substantive issue gets the state law of the state where the federal court sits. A procedural issue gets the Federal Rules.
- **Erie is always a federal-court question,** and per the professor it "only comes up if the fed court can hear it due to **diversity**." For her exam, pick "diversity" (her 2025 Q17 key agrees).

## Other exam cues from the same class
- **If the question asks only about subject matter jurisdiction, answer only SMJ.**
- **A corporation sued in its state of incorporation is always subject to personal jurisdiction there** (it's at home there).
- **You need both personal jurisdiction and subject matter jurisdiction.**
- **State courts have SMJ over about 99% of cases.** The exception is when federal law makes federal jurisdiction exclusive.
- **Federal question and diversity are the only ways a case can be removed to federal court.**
- **For joinder questions, run the SMJ analysis for each claim.**
- **A federal question must appear in the plaintiff's complaint** (the well-pleaded complaint rule).

## How these will be used in question sets
- Erie questions will test *why* (forum shopping, uniformity) and *which test helps which party*. They won't ask you to apply a test to new facts.
- 2–4 Erie questions per full practice exam; 1–2 per shorter set.
